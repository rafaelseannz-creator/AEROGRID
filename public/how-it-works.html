AeroGrid · solid-state cooling

How AeroGrid cools a room without a compressor

A ceramic plate heats up when an electric field lines up its atomic dipoles, and turns cold when the field is switched off. AeroGrid throws the heat outside and keeps the cold. Press play to follow one full cycle.

The four phases

What each part does

Electrocaloric ceramic plate
The working material. It replaces the chemical refrigerant: it heats when its dipoles align and cools when they scatter.
Electrodes and high-voltage driver
Apply and remove the electric field across the plate. Switching them is the only thing that drives the cycle, so there is no compressor.
Magnetocaloric layer and sliding magnet*
The hybrid part. The magnet slides under the plate whenever the electric field is on, so this layer heats and cools in step with the ceramic and adds to its effect.
Fan
The only moving air part. It runs during phases 2 and 4 and rests while the field is switching.
Diverter valve
One flap with two positions. It sends air outside when the plate is hot and back into the room when the plate is cold.
Slim window vent strip*
A flat exhaust that sits in a window opened a crack, in place of a bulky hose. Nothing is drilled or installed, so it suits a rental.

* Concept design choices made for this diagram: the sliding magnet with its magnetocaloric layer, and the window vent strip. Temperatures and timings are illustrative and the cycle is slowed down so each step can be seen.
